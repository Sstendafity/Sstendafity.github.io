WhatsApp customer service for an Indonesian small business · Python, FastAPI, Groq · August 2026

An Indonesian seller answers roughly 200 WhatsApp messages a day, and most of them repeat four questions: is it in stock, how much, where is my order, how do I pay. This answers those from the seller's own catalogue, FAQ and order records, and refuses everything else.

Eval accuracy
98%79 of 81 as scored, 81 of 81 after two scorer bugs were fixed
Traps refused

Retrieval

Three decisions carry the reliability

  1. Rules decide, the model only writes. Intent classification, order-ID parsing and escalation are deterministic regex rules. They are testable, free, and incapable of inventing anything. The model is used only to phrase an answer over facts already chosen for it.
  2. The model sees the retrieved rows and nothing else. Its own world knowledge is never the source of a price, a stock level, a policy or a delivery date.
  3. When retrieval finds nothing, the model is never called. A fixed refusal plus a human handoff goes back instead. A model that is not asked cannot invent.

No vector database and no embedding service: the knowledge base is 33 short documents, and an in-memory TF-IDF index built from the standard library retrieves as well as anything heavier, installs nothing and runs offline. The upgrade path is marked in the code for the day that stops being true.

The suite is written to break the assistant, not to flatter it. A case fails when the bot invents an answer and also when it refuses one that is sitting in its own context. Over-refusal loses a sale as surely as a hallucination loses trust.

What the evaluation actually found

Six of the failures encountered across every run were bugs in my scoring code, not in the assistant. A valid refusal was being marked wrong because its verb was missing from a synonym list. So the scorer got its own regression test, and the refusal check was rewritten as a pattern rather than a list that grows one bug report at a time. The corrected scorer still fails an invented price, so the check was widened and not weakened.

Manual use then found a defect the suite had missed: lowering the model's reasoning effort made it start refusing a payment question whose answer was in its retrieved context. That became two permanent over-refusal cases. The behaviour holds across two different models, which is the part that matters: the reliability comes from the retrieval and rules design, not from one model's good manners.

Stated plainly

This runs on synthetic data behind a simulated WhatsApp interface, not the WhatsApp Business API and not real customer traffic. Real buyers send voice notes, photographs, half-finished sentences and mixed Sundanese. The suite covers the failure modes I could anticipate. What transfers is the loop: every defect found becomes a permanent case before the fix is called done.

MaleoScribe: audio to structured clinical documentation

Technical owner · published, first author

An end-to-end LLM pipeline turning raw consultation audio into structured clinical documentation in the SOAPIE format, in Indonesian: ASR transcription and correction, structured extraction against a typed schema, and document generation, behind a production interface.

I designed and ran the evaluation, which became the published contribution. The argument was that a single accuracy number does not tell you whether a scribe is safe to deploy, so the protocol measured four things instead.

Word error rate
0.102character error rate 0.072
Semantic similarity
0.978cosine, against reference documentation
  • Semantic fidelity alongside raw error rate. A note can be word-imperfect and still clinically correct, or word-perfect and clinically wrong.
  • Domain-specific error. Medical-term and entity error rates, scoring the words that matter separately from filler.
  • Noise robustness across controlled SNR levels, because a clinic is not a quiet room.
  • Repeatability. Output stability across repeated runs on identical input, because a scribe that returns a different note each time cannot be trusted with a medical record.

Designed with an explicit clinical safety boundary: the system summarises facts for physician review and does not diagnose.

Compliance auditing and anomaly detection

Isolation Forest with a retrieval-augmented contextualizer · top-5 candidate in a national public-sector AI track

Flags irregularities in large, messy regulatory datasets. The anomaly model scores each record; the retrieval layer attaches the specific regulation it appears to violate, so a reviewer gets a reason rather than an unexplained score they have to take on faith.

Shipped with the reviewer-facing application: a risk-sorted audit queue, per-record deep dive, facility benchmarking and risk heatmaps. Personally identifying columns are dropped at load and never reach the cache; the analysis runs on anonymised identifiers, codes and aggregates.

Code-prediction and billing engine

Claims pipeline for the national casemix reimbursement system

Engineering across the pipeline: model retraining, routing-logic fixes, and an anomaly-detection layer that catches data irregularities before they reach customers.

Prediction and tariff run local and fast. Regulatory retrieval and the LLM plausibility audit run upstream and slow. The service is shaped around that split, so a coder gets the fast result immediately and the audit follows.

Medical document extraction pipeline

Arbitrary medical PDFs to typed, de-identified, structured data

  1. Ingestion and triage. Per-page classification into native-text against scanned, using a structural text-quality score that rejects corrupt text layers rather than only empty ones. Several table extractors run in parallel, each result recorded with its accuracy and bounding box.
  2. OCR. Pluggable engines recover the scanned pages back into the same document representation.
  3. De-identification. Rule-based patterns plus named-entity recognition tuned for Indonesian, redacting the text layer, the table cells and the OCR word bounding boxes, so scanned images can be blacked out downstream.
  4. Structured extraction. LLM tool-use against a typed schema, run only on de-identified input.

Two decisions carried the design. Every phase reads and writes one intermediate representation carrying page and bounding-box provenance end to end, so any field in the output traces back to the pixel it came from. And every dependency was chosen to be commercial-license-safe; the obvious PDF library was rejected on licence grounds. It ships with a batch audit tool that flags the pages needing human review before the output is trusted.

How I decide something is ready

The same argument runs through a fiber sensor, a clinical scribe and a shop assistant: one number is not evidence. Each of these systems shipped because a protocol said it could, and the protocol was designed to say no.

Measure more than one thing

A clinical scribe was scored on transcription error, semantic fidelity, medical-term error, noise robustness and repeatability. A note can be word-perfect and clinically wrong. Word error rate alone would have cleared it.

Write the test to fail

Eighty-one cases built from slang, typos, prompt injection and questions with no answer in the data. Cases fail in both directions, for inventing an answer and for refusing one that was present. Six of the bugs found were in the scorer, so the scorer got its own tests.

Calibrate against a gold standard

An optical cardiac sensor was validated against simultaneous ECG, not against its own repeatability. The packaged sensor held 2.29% error, and statistical testing confirmed the enclosure cost nothing in measurement quality.

Fiber-optic cardiac sensing

Singlemode to multimode to singlemode fiber structure as the transducer · published, national research grant

Optical sensing for non-invasive cardiac monitoring, far cheaper and simpler than the fiber Bragg gratings normally used for it, and immune to the electromagnetic interference that rules out electrode-based monitoring inside an MRI bore.

Cardiac vibration strains the fiber, changing its refractive index through the photoelastic effect and modulating transmitted optical power. A heartbeat read as an intensity signal on a simple power meter, with no spectrometer in the setup. The contribution was showing the sensor could be mechanically packaged without losing accuracy, validated against simultaneous ECG.

A second grant-funded thread covered fetal phonocardiography: sensor fabrication across a full variant matrix of step-index, graded-index and coreless multimode sections at 3 cm and 4.5 cm, spliced between single-mode fiber and interrogated at 1550 nm. Every variant was tested on an acoustic rig replaying a public fetal heart sound database, so each met an identical, repeatable input.

Finding: the 3 cm coreless variant resolved the first and second heart sounds most clearly. Graded-index was markedly quieter, but its amplitude was too small to segment reliably. A real sensitivity against noise trade-off, documented rather than smoothed over.

Separately, on the KardiaQ portable screening initiative, I wrote the pipelines that clean noisy smartphone-acquired seismocardiogram and phonocardiogram signals. Consumer-hardware recordings arrive with irregular sampling and heavy motion artefact, so the pipeline derives its own sample rate from the timestamps rather than trusting a nominal value, then bandpass-filters, denoises and detects beats.
